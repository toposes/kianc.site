4 Local Model Structure

Homotopical preliminaries

For this section, suppose that we work in an arbitrary model category.

Lemma 4.1.

Let f:X→Y be any fibration. Then there is a fibration f′:X′→Y′ where X′ and Y′ are fibrant replacements of X and Y respectively.
Lemma 4.2.

Suppose we have a commutative cube such that the front, back, and right sides are homotopy pullback squares. Then so is the left side.

Lemma 4.3.

Homotopy does not depend on choice of a cylinder object. Let X and Y be any objects, and suppose Z is a cylinder object for X.

X+X↣Z↠X

Let f,g:X⇉Y be parallel arrows and h:Z→Y be a homotopy between f and g.

Suppose Z′ is another cylinder object for X. Then we can find a homotopy h′:Z′→Y between f and g.

Lemma 4.4.

A weak equivalence between fibrations is a homotopy equivalence.

Lemma 4.5.

Quillen equivalence fact If F⊣G is Quillen with G preserving and reflecting weqs and the unit is pointwise weq then Quillen equiv.

4.1 Setup

For the rest of the section, we work in the following situation.

Let F⊣G⊣H be a triple of adjoints between model categories 𝒞 and 𝒟 with G:𝒟→𝒞. We shall also assume:

  1. (a)

    G is both left and right Quillen.

  2. (b)

    Cofibrations in 𝒞 and 𝒟 are monomorphisms. In particular, all objects are cofibrant in both 𝒞 and 𝒟.

  3. (c)

    For all (cofibrant) object X∈𝒞, X→G⁢F⁢X is a weak equivalence. For all fibrant X∈𝒞, G⁢H⁢X→X is a weak equivalence.

  4. (d)

    G is conservative, in particular faithful, and the unit 1→H⁢G is pointwise monic.

Notation.

Let W denote the class G−1⁢[𝖶𝖾𝗊⁢(𝒞)], and FW denote the class of maps with right lifting property against 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W.

Proposition 4.6.

(𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟),W,FW) is a model structure.

We will shortly prove Prop 4.6 in §4.2.

Notation.

Let 𝒟W be the model category described by (𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟),W,FW). To distinguish the model categories 𝒟 and 𝒟W, we will sometimes say local fibrations or local fibrant objects to mean fibrations and fibrant objects in 𝒟W.

4.2 Existence of model structure

Observation.

Since G is left Quillen, it preserves weak equivalences between cofibrant objects. Since all objects in 𝒟 are cofibrant, 𝖶𝖾𝗊⁢(𝒟)⊆W. Also, because G is a right adjoint and faithful, G preserves and reflects monomorphisms. Thus, G−1⁢[𝖳𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒞)]=𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W.

Observation.

W satisfies 3-for-2 as 𝒞 is a model category. Since G is right Quillen, W is stable under homotopy pullbacks.

Observation.

Since 𝒟 is a model category, (𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟),𝖳𝖥𝗂𝖻⁢(𝒟)) is already a weak factorization system. Hence it suffices to check that (𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W,FW) is a weak factorization system and that FW∩W=𝖳𝖥𝗂𝖻⁢(𝒟).

Lemma 4.7.

H⁢[𝖥𝗂𝖻⁢(𝒞)]⊂FW.

Proof.

Let f be a fibration in 𝒞. We want H⁢f to have right lifting against 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W. Suppose c:A→B is in 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W, which means G⁢c is a trivial cofibration in 𝒞. Then G⁢c⋔f since f is a fibration in 𝒞, which is equivalent to c⋔H⁢f by adjunction. ∎

Lemma 4.8.

Let f:X→Y be any fibration. Let P→Y be the pullback of H⁢G⁢f along the unit map Y→H⁢G⁢Y.

Then the comparison map X→P is in W.

Proof.

The simple case is when f:X→Y is a fibration between fibrant objects. Since X and Y are fibrant, we have that the unit maps X→H⁢G⁢X and Y→H⁢G⁢Y are in W. Consider the unit square f→H⁢G⁢f and the pullback P→Y.

Then P→H⁢G⁢X is in W since it is a pullback of Y→H⁢G⁢Y which is in W. We deduce that X→P is in W by 3-for-2 of W.

Now, suppose that f is any fibration. Let f′:X′→Y′ be a replacement of f by a fibration between fibrant objects.

Consider the cube formed by the unit squares for f and f′. Let P→Y and P′→Y′ be the pullbacks of H⁢G⁢f and H⁢G⁢f′ along the corresponding unit maps.

(1)

In the diagram, arrows in W are marked with a circle and weak equivalences are marked with a bar. Left face of the cube (red) is a homotopy pullback. Since H and G are right Quillen, right face of the cube (blue) is also a homotopy pullback [??] since the vertical maps are fibrations. Then so is the square formed between P→Y and P′→Y′. By pasting lemma, we deduce that the square formed by X→P and X′→P′ is also a homotopy pullback. Since X′→P′ is in W, so is X→P. ∎

Lemma 4.9.

(𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W,FW) is a weak factorization system.

Proof.

We first show that any fibration f:X→Y can be factored into a map in 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W followed by a map in FW.

By the previous lemma, X→P is in W. X→P is a monomorphism since the unit map X→H⁢G⁢X is a monomorphism and factors through it. Hence X→P→Y is a factorization of f into a map in 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W followed by a map in FW.

If f:A→X is any map, then we can first factor it into a trivial cofibration followed by a fibration. Since 𝖳𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)⊆𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W, we finish by factoring the fibration part as above.

It remains to check that 𝖢𝗈𝖿(𝒟)∩W=(FW)⋔. Since 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W=G−1⁢[𝖳𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒞)], it is enough to show that if a cofibration c:A→B has left lifting against FW, then G⁢c is a trivial cofibration in 𝒞. We make use the fact that H⁢[𝖥𝗂𝖻⁢(𝒞)]⊂FW: c⋔FW implies c⋔H⁢[𝖥𝗂𝖻⁢(𝒞)]. By adjunction, c⋔H⁢[𝖥𝗂𝖻⁢(𝒞)] iff G⁢c⋔𝖥𝗂𝖻⁢(𝒞). That is, G⁢c is a trivial cofibration. ∎

Lemma 4.10.

FW∩W=𝖳𝖥𝗂𝖻⁢(𝒟).

Proof.

Let f:X→Y be in FW∩W. Then we can factor f into a cofibration followed by a trivial fibration using the model structure on 𝒟. Since 𝖶𝖾𝗊⁢(𝒟)⊆W, the cofibration is in W by 3-for-2. Then f is a retract of the trivial fibration by the retract lemma, since it right lifts against 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W. ∎

Observation.

𝗂𝖽:𝒟→𝒟W is left Quillen.

Proposition 4.11.

F⊣G is a Quillen equivalence between 𝒞 and 𝒟W.

Proof.

G preserves and reflects weak equivalences and unit maps X→G⁢F⁢X are weak equivalences [??]. ∎

4.3 Local fibrations

Suppose these additional hypotheses:

  1. (e)

    The underlying 1-categories of 𝒞 and 𝒟 are Grothendieck 1-toposes.

  2. (f)

    𝒞 and 𝒟 both have universes for fibrations, Frobenius property (and therefore right proper), equivalence extension property, and fibration extension property.

The goal of this section is to prove:

Proposition 4.12.

𝒟W has universes for fibrations, Frobenius property, equivalence extension property, and fibration extension property.

Lemma 4.13.

Let f:A→X be any map. f is a homotopy equivalence in 𝒟W if and only if f is a homotopy equivalence in 𝒟.

Proof.

A cylinder object in 𝒟 is still a cylinder object in 𝒟W. ∎

Lemma 4.14.

Let f0:X0→Y and f1:X1→Y be weakly equivalent fibrations over Y. Then f0 is local if and only if f1 is.

Lemma 4.15.

Let Y be a fibrant object in 𝒟; f:X→Y and f′:X′→Y be in FW; and g:X→X′ is such that f=f′⁢g. Then g is in W iff it is a weak equivalence.
Proof.

Since f and f′ are fibrations in 𝒟W and g a weak equivalence in 𝒟W, g must be a homotopy equivalence in 𝒟W and therefore in 𝒟. ∎

Proposition 4.16.

Let f be any fibration in 𝒟. f is in FW if and only if the unit square f→H⁢G⁢f is a homotopy pullback in 𝒟.

Proof.

(⇒) Suppose f is in FW. Let P→Y be the pullback of H⁢G⁢f along Y→H⁢G⁢Y. The comparison map X→P is in W by Lemma 4.8. Since both f and P→Y are in FW, X→P is a weak equivalence.

(⇐) Suppose f→H⁢G⁢f is a homotopy pullback square. Since X and P are both homotopy pullbacks, the comparison map X→P is a weak equivalence in 𝒟. Since X→P is also monic, it is a trivial cofibration. Then f is a retract of P→Y which is in FW. ∎

Notation.

Define a functorial factorization on 𝒟 as follows. Given any f:X→Y, f can be factored into X→λ⁢fE⁢f→ρ⁢fY

where ρ⁢f:E⁢f→Y is the pullback of H⁢G⁢f along ηY and λ⁢f:X→E⁢f is the comparison map.

Lemma 4.17.

This functorial factorization is cartesian.

Proof.

Suppose we have a pullback square f→f′. By pasting lemma, it is enough to show that ρ⁢f→ρ⁢f′ is a pullback. Since G and H are right adjoints, H⁢G⁢f→H⁢G⁢f′ is also a pullback square. ρ⁢f and ρ⁢f′ are pullbacks of H⁢G⁢f and H⁢G⁢f′ along ηY and ηY′.

Since the back, front, and right faces of the cube are pullbacks, so is the left face. ∎

Proposition 4.18.

Let f:X→Y be any fibration in 𝒟. f is in FW if and only if λ⁢f:f→ρ⁢f has a retraction.

Proof.

λ⁢f is in 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W and since f is a fibration, ρ⁢f is in FW. If f is in FW, then it right lifts against λ⁢f. Then by the retract lemma λ⁢f has a retraction. On the other hand, if λ⁢f has a retraction, then f is in FW since FW is closed under retracts. ∎

Observation.

Let f be in FW. Then λ⁢f is a weak equivalence by 4.16.

Lemma 4.19.

Let f′:X′→Y′ be in FW. Suppose f:X→Y is a pullback of f′:X′→Y′ along a monomorphism Y→Y′.

Let r:E⁢f→X be a retraction of λ⁢f over Y. Then there is a retraction r′:E⁢f′→X of λ⁢f′ over Y′ such that r is the pullback of r′ along X→X′.
Proof.

Since f′ and f are both in FW, λ⁢f′ and λ⁢f are both trivial cofibrations. Let Q be the pushout of λ⁢f along i. Since λ⁢f→λ⁢f′ is a pullback, Q→E⁢f′ is a monomorphism as Q is a union of subobjects.

The map X′→Q is a trivial cofibration since it is a pushout of λ⁢f. Then Q→E⁢f′ is a trivial cofibration by 3-for-2. Since f′ is a fibration, it right-lifts against Q→E⁢f′.

Take r′:E⁢f′→X′ to be a solution to the lifting problem. ∎

Lemma 4.20.

For each f:X→Y in 𝒟, there is a morphism 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f)→Y whose sections correspond to retractions of λ⁢f:f→ρ⁢f.

Proof.

Using the lcc structure, we can define [ρ⁢f,f]:D1→Y and [f,f]:D2→Y.

Let 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f) be the pullback of the cospan. Elements of the fiber of −∘λf:[ρf,f]→[f,f] over 𝗂𝖽X:f→f, and thus sections of 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f)→Y, correspond to retractions of λ⁢f. ∎

Lemma 4.21.

Let f:X→Y and g:Y′→Y be in 𝒟. Then 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(g∗⁢f)→Y′ is the pullback of 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f)→Y along g, i.e., 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(g∗⁢f)=g∗⁢(𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f)).
Proof.

The functorial factorization f↦(λ⁢f,ρ⁢f) is cartesian, so λ⁢(g∗⁢f)=g∗⁢(λ⁢f) and ρ⁢(g∗⁢f)=g∗⁢(ρ⁢f). The result follows as g∗:𝒟/Y→𝒟/Y′ is a cartesian closed functor. ∎

Lemma 4.22.

Let f:X→Y be any map. Let f′:X′→Y′=𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f) be the pullback of f along g:𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f)→Y. Then 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f′)→Y′ has a canonical section.

Proof.

That is, f′=g∗⁢f so by 4.21, 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f′)=g∗⁢(𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f)).

The canonical section 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f)→g∗⁢(𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f)) is induced by the identity on 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f). ∎

Proposition 4.23.

𝒟W has universes for fibrations. That is, there are universes for local fibrations.

Proof.

Let π:U˙→U be a universe for fibrations in 𝒟. Define UW:=𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(π), and let πW:UW˙→UW be the pullback of π along UW→U. By 4.22, πW is a local fibration.

We claim that πW:UW˙→UW is the universe for local fibrations. We have to check

  1. (i)

    Given a local fibration f:X→Y, we can find ⌜⁢f⁢⌝:Y→UW such that f is the pullback of πW along ⌜⁢f⁢⌝.

  2. (ii)

    UW satisfies realignment.

Let f:X→Y be a local fibration. Then in particular f is a fibration, so it is classified by some ⌜⁢f⁢⌝:Y→U such that f=⌜⁢f⁢⌝∗⁢(π).

For (i), it suffices to show that ⌜⁢f⁢⌝ factors through UW. Since f is a local fibration, there is a section Y→𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f) and thus a Y→UW making the bottom triangle commute. As f=⌜⁢f⁢⌝∗⁢(π), 𝖱𝖾𝗍𝗋λ⁢(f) is the pullback of UW along ⌜⁢f⁢⌝.

Let f:X→Y be a local fibration and f′:X′→Y′ be the pullback of f along some monomorphism m:Y′→Y. Suppose f′=g∗⁢πW for some g:Y′→UW. We want to find h:Y→UW such that g=h⁢m and f=h∗⁢πW.

Let g′ be the composite Y′→𝑔UW→U. By pasting lemma, f′ is the pullback of π along this map. Since π:U˙→U has realignment property, we can find k:Y→U such that g′=k⁢m and f=k∗⁢π.

As f is a local fibration, k factors through UW via some h:Y→UW. Then f:X→Y is a pullback of πW along h. ∎

Proposition 4.24.

𝒟W has the Frobenius property.

Proof.

We have to show that the class 𝖳𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟W)=𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W is stable under pullbacks along local fibrations. Let c:A→B be in 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W and g:B′→B be a local fibration.

We need to show that g∗⁢c is in 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W. Since 𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W=G−1⁢[𝖳𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒞)], we show that G⁢(g∗⁢c) is a trivial cofibration in 𝒞. G preserves pullbacks and (all) fibrations in 𝒟, so G⁢g is a fibration in 𝒞 and G⁢(g∗⁢c)=(G⁢g)∗⁢(G⁢c). We have G⁢c∈𝖳𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒞) since c∈𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W.

𝒞 has the Frobenius property, giving us G⁢(g∗⁢c)∈𝖳𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒞). ∎

Proposition 4.25.

𝒟W has the equivalence extension property.

Proof.

If a map w:f0→f1 between local fibrations f0 and f1 over an object A is in W, then it is a homotopy equivalence in both 𝒟W and 𝒟. Therefore it is a weak equivalence in 𝒟. Suppose we have a cofibration c:A→B and a local fibration g1 over B such that f1=c∗⁢g1. By the equivalence extension property for 𝒟, we have a weak equivalence z:g0→g1 extending w where g0∈𝖥𝗂𝖻⁢(𝒟). But g0 is local if and only if g1 is. ∎

Proposition 4.26.

𝒟W has the fibration extension property.

Proof.

Let c:A→B be in 𝖳𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟W)=𝖢𝗈𝖿⁢(𝒟)∩W and p:X→A be a local fibration. We have to find a local fibration q:Y→B extending p, i.e. p is the pullback of q along c.

G⁢c is a trivial cofibration and G⁢p is a fibration (in 𝒞). Using the fibration extension property for 𝒞, we can find f:Z→G⁢B such that G⁢p=(G⁢c)∗⁢f.

H preserves pullbacks and fibrations, so H⁢G⁢p=(H⁢G⁢c)∗⁢(H⁢f).

Let p0:P→A be the pullback of H⁢G⁢p along ηA:A→H⁢G⁢A and q0 be the pullback of H⁢f along ηB:B→H⁢G⁢B. We obtain the following commuting cube:

Note that vertical maps of the cube are local fibrations. Since the front, left, and right faces of the cube are pullbacks, so is the back. That is, p0=c∗⁢q0.

Recall that p:X→A is a local fibration. Then the comparison map X→P is a weak equivalence. Then by 4.25 (equivalence extension property for 𝒟W) we can find a local fibration q:Y→B such that p=c∗⁢q. ∎

2 Weak Orthogonality

2.1 Definitions

Definition 2.1 (Lifting problem).

In any ambient category, a commuting square is a lifting problem when we ask whether there is a diagonal lift.

A solution to the lifting problem given by the square (h,k):f→g is a morphism j:B→X making the triangles commute. That is, j∘f=h and g∘j=k.

Definition 2.2 (Lifting property).

Let 𝒞 be a category and ℳ,𝒩 be classes of morphisms in 𝒞. Let f,g∈𝒞→ be any morphisms.

  1. (i)

    We write f⋔g when every lifting problem of the following shape admits a solution.
  2. (ii)

    We say that f left lifts against 𝒩 and write f⋔𝒩 when f⋔h for all h∈𝒩.

  3. (iii)

    We say that g right lifts against ℳ and write ℳ⋔g when k⋔g for all k∈ℳ.

  4. (iv)

    We write ℳ⋔𝒩 when every k∈ℳ has left lifting against 𝒩 and every h∈𝒩 has right lifting against ℳ. That is, every lifting problem of the following shape admits a solution.
  5. (v)

    Let 𝒩⋔ denote the class of morphisms which left lift against 𝒩. Similarly, let ℳ⋔ denote the class of morphisms which right lift against ℳ.

Remark 2.3.

That is, 𝒩⋔ is the largest class of morphisms with left lifting against 𝒩, so if ℳ⋔𝒩, then ℳ⊆𝒩⋔. Symmetrically, ℳ⋔ is the largest class of morphisms with right lifting against ℳ, so if ℳ⋔𝒩, then 𝒩⊆ℳ⋔.

Definition 2.4 (Lifting structure).

Let 𝒞 be a category and I:𝕀→𝒞→. Let f:X→Y be a morphism in 𝒞. Then we have a functor ψ:(𝖼𝗈𝖽∘I)/X→I/f sending the diagonal map below to the commutative square.

We say that f:X→Y has a I⧄-structure if ψ has a section φ:I/f→(𝖼𝗈𝖽∘I)/X.

Concretely, this means that for any commutative square (h,k):I⁢(i)→f there is a chosen diagonal filler φ⁢(i,h,k), such that for any morphism w:j→i in I with I⁢(w)=(u,v), we have φ⁢(j,h⁢u,k⁢v)=φ⁢(i,h,k)∘v.

We write f∈I⧄ when f has a I⧄-structure, where I⧄ is the full subcategory of 𝒞→ of morphisms with I⧄-structures.

Definition 2.5 (Weak factorization system).

Let ℒ,ℛ be classes of morphisms in 𝒞. (ℒ,ℛ) is said to be a weak factorization system (wfs) if the following hold:

  1. (i)

    Every morphism in 𝒞 factors into a map in ℒ followed by a map in ℛ.

  2. (ii)

    ℒ⋔ℛ, i.e., the classes are weakly orthogonal to one another.

  3. (iii)

    ℒ⋔=ℛ and ℛ⋔=ℒ, and the classes ℒ and ℛ are closed under retracts.

2.2 Factorization

Proposition 2.6.

Let 𝒞 be a category, I:𝕀→𝒞→ and J:𝕁→𝒞→. If I factors through J, then J⧄⊆I⧄.
Proof.

Suppose f:X→Y has a J⧄-structure φJ:J/f→(𝖼𝗈𝖽∘J)/X. Then we can define an I⧄-structure φI on f by taking φI⁢(i,h,k)=φJ⁢(L⁢i,h,k), since I⁢(i)=J⁢(L⁢i). ∎

Corollary 2.7.

Let F:𝒞→𝒟 be a functor. Suppose I:𝕀→𝒞→ and J:𝕁→𝒟→. Suppose F⁢I:𝕀→𝒟→ factors through J. Then J⧄⊆(F⁢I)⧄.
Proposition 2.8.

Let F⊣G be an adjunction where F:𝒞→𝒟. Let 𝒮 be a collection of maps in 𝒞. Let f be a morphism in 𝒟. Then F⁢(𝒮)⋔f iff 𝒮⋔G⁢f.

Proof.

Let g be any morphism in 𝒞. We have that F⁢g⋔f iff g⋔G⁢f by adjunction. ∎

Proposition 2.9.

Let F⊣G with F:𝒞→𝒟. Suppose I:𝕀→𝒞→. Then (F⁢I)⧄=G−1⁢(I⧄).

Proof.

Let f be a morphism in 𝒟 such that G⁢f has a I⧄-structure φ. Then define a (F⁢I)⧄-structure ρ on f by taking ρ⁢(i,h,k)=φ⁢(i,h~,k~), where (h~,k~):I⁢(i)→f is the transpose of (h,k):F⁢I⁢(i)→f given by the adjunction. Similarly, given that f has an (F⁢I)⧄-structure, we can define an I⧄-structure on G⁢f. ∎

Proposition 2.10.

Let I:𝕀→𝒞 and (ℒ,ℛ) be a wfs on 𝒞 such that ℛ=I⧄. Let J:𝕁→𝒟 and (ℒ′,ℛ′) be a wfs on 𝒟 such that ℛ′=J⧄. Suppose F:𝒞→𝒟 is such that F⁢I factors through J. Then F⁢(ℒ)⊆ℒ′.

2.10 yields a fact that can be used to show a functor is left Quillen.

Corollary 2.11.

Let 𝒞 and 𝒟 be model categories. Let I:𝕀→𝒞→ be such that I⧄=𝖥𝗂𝖻𝒞 and let J:𝕁→𝒟→ be such that J⧄=𝖥𝗂𝖻𝒟. Suppose F:𝒞→𝒟 is such that F⁢I factors through J. Then F⁢(𝖳𝖢𝗈𝖿𝒞)⊆𝖳𝖢𝗈𝖿𝒟.

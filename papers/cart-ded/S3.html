3 Motivating Example

3.1 Cube categories

Definition 3.1 (Cartesian cube category, ℂ).

The Cartesian cube category, denoted ℂ, is the Lawvere theory with constants 0, 1, and no other operations (the theory of bipointed objects, the initial Lawvere theory with two constants.) A substitution

(x1,…,xm∣a1,…,an)

is a morphism in ℂ if ai is a term in the language with only constants 0 and 1, which means precisely that ai∈{0,x1,…,xm,1}.

Definition 3.2 (Dedekind cube category, 𝔻).

The Dedekind cube category, denoted 𝔻, is the Lawvere theory of bounded distributive lattices. It has two binary operations, ∧ (meet) and ∨ (join), called connections, which are both commutative, associative, idempotent and subject to these additional axioms:

  • •

    identity: a∨0=a and a∧1=a

  • •

    absorption: a∨(a∧b)=a and a∧(a∨b)=a

  • •

    distributivity: a∨(b∧c)=(a∨b)∧(a∨c) and a∧(b∨c)=(a∧b)∨(b∧c)

A substitution (x1,…,xm∣a1,…,an) is a morphism in 𝔻 if ai is a term in this larger language. Two morphisms (x1,…,xm∣a1,…,an), (x1,…,xm∣b1,…,bn) are identified whenever ai and bi are provably equal under the axioms.

Remark.

In ℂ and 𝔻, we have [n]=[1]n as they are Lawvere theories.

Construction 3.3 (Canonical inclusions).

We have an identity-on-objects inclusion

j:ℂ→𝔻

sending a morphism (x1,…,xm∣a1,…,an):[m]→[n] in ℂ to the morphism presented by the same substitution (now seen as terms in the expanded language with connections) in 𝔻. This assignment is faithful because different variables and constants cannot become provably equal, even under the presence of connections.

Remark.

𝔻 can be any of the other cartesian cubical categories. Any Lawvere theory with constants 0 and 1 will work here, including join semi-lattices or cubes with negations. We use the Dedekind cube category for 𝔻 with the aim of constructing a model structure on 𝔻^ which presents spaces.

Proposition 3.5.

j∗ is conservative. In particular, it is faithful.

Proof.

The canonical inclusion j:ℂ→𝔻 is identity on objects, in particular surjective on objects. Hence, the restriction j∗ is conservative. ∎

3.3 Left Quillen

Proposition 3.9.

j∗:𝔻^𝗂𝗇𝗍→ℂ^𝗂𝗇𝗍 is left Quillen.

Proof.

𝔻^𝗂𝗇𝗍=𝔻^𝖼𝗂𝗌 since δ is a retract of an anodyne cofibration in 𝔻^. Anodyne cofibrations in ℂ^ are trivial cofibrations in ℂ^𝗂𝗇𝗍. Any s:A→B exhibiting A as a strong deformation retract of B is anodyne. j∗⁢δ0:1→j∗⁢𝖩 is a strong deformation retract of j∗⁢𝖩, hence it is a trivial cofibration in ℂ^𝗂𝗇𝗍. Then j∗:𝔻^𝖼𝗂𝗌→ℂ^𝗂𝗇𝗍 is left Quillen. Since 𝔻^𝗂𝗇𝗍=𝔻^𝖼𝗂𝗌, j∗ is left Quillen between the interval model structures. ∎
